# 03 — Accessibility Q&A

**A1. What is WCAG?**
> "Web Content Accessibility Guidelines. It defines success criteria under four principles —
> Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, Robust — at levels A, AA, and AAA. Most organizations target
> AA."

**A2. What are the POUR principles?**
> "Perceivable: users can perceive the content. Operable: they can use the controls. Understandable:
> the content/behavior is clear. Robust: it works with assistive technologies."

**A3. How do you test accessibility?**
> "A mix: automated axe scans for mechanical issues, keyboard-only navigation, screen-reader passes for
> critical journeys, contrast/zoom/motion checks, and manual review. Automation alone is not enough."

**A4. What does axe catch?**
> "Missing alt text, labels, contrast, invalid ARIA, some heading/landmark issues. It does not catch
> whether the alt text is meaningful, whether the flow makes sense, or whether a screen-reader user can
> complete the task."

**A5. What is the most common accessibility bug?**
> "Non-semantic interactive elements — a `div` with `onClick` instead of a `button` — which breaks
> keyboard access and screen-reader semantics. Also missing labels and focus outlines."

**A6. How do you test keyboard accessibility?**
> "Tab through every interactive element, confirm focus is visible and in logical order, activate with
> Enter/Space, test modals for focus trap/return, and confirm there is no keyboard trap."

**A7. How do you test a modal?**
> "Open it with the keyboard, confirm focus moves inside, Tab stays inside, Escape closes it, and focus
> returns to the trigger. Check `role="dialog"`/`aria-modal` and the accessible name."

**A8. How do you test forms?**
> "Every input has a label, required state is programmatic, errors are associated with the field,
> messages are specific, and the form is usable by keyboard and screen reader."

**A9. What is the contrast requirement?**
> "4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and UI components/graphics (WCAG AA)."

**A10. What is ARIA?**
> "Accessible Rich Internet Applications: roles, states, and properties for custom widgets. Prefer
> native HTML; bad ARIA is worse than no ARIA."

**A11. How do you test dynamic content?**
> "Use live regions for status/errors, confirm announcements with a screen reader, and test loading/
> success/error states. Automated tools rarely catch this."

**A12. How do you test mobile accessibility?**
> "TalkBack/VoiceOver, touch target size, font scaling, orientation, contrast, and focus order. Use
> platform accessibility inspectors."

**A13. How do you integrate accessibility into CI?**
> "axe scan on critical pages/components, fail on new critical/serious issues, track existing ones with
> owner/expiry, and keep keyboard/screen-reader checks in the release checklist."

**A14. Is a Lighthouse 100 accessibility score enough?**
> "No. It is a useful signal, but it does not prove keyboard or screen-reader usability. Manual testing
> is still required."

**A15. How do you prioritize accessibility fixes?**
> "By impact and severity: blockers for critical journeys first (can't log in, can't pay), then serious
> issues, then moderate. Legal/compliance risk also raises priority."

**A16. What is reduced motion?**
> "A user preference (`prefers-reduced-motion`) to minimize animation. Respect it for transitions,
> parallax, and auto-playing motion."

**A17. How do you test color-only information?**
> "Check that status/errors are conveyed by text/icon/shape, not color alone. Use a grayscale or
> color-blind simulation."

**A18. What is an accessible name?**
> "The text a screen reader announces for a control — from a label, `aria-label`, `aria-labelledby`, or
> content. Every interactive element needs one."

**A19. How do you handle third-party widgets?**
> "Test them like your own: keyboard, screen reader, semantics. If they fail, wrap/replace or document
> the risk with an owner."

**A20. What would you automate first?**
> "axe on the critical pages and components, with a gate on new critical/serious violations. Then add
> keyboard checks for critical journeys, and keep screen-reader review manual."
