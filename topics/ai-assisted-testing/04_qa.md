# 04 — AI-Assisted Testing Q&A

**A1. How do you use AI in testing?**
> "For drafting and acceleration: test ideas, boilerplate page objects/API clients, synthetic data,
> failure summarization, and refactoring suggestions. I always review, run, and validate the output.
> The strategy and risk decisions stay with me."

**A2. Can AI replace testers?**
> "No. It can reduce boilerplate and surface ideas, but it doesn't know the business risk, can't
> validate its own output against the real system, and can't own quality decisions. It shifts effort
> from writing to reviewing and designing."

**A3. How do you validate AI-generated tests?**
> "Compile/run them, check the spec/fields, verify locators against the DOM, review assertions for
> meaningfulness, and add domain edge cases. Generated tests are drafts until proven."

**A4. What are the biggest risks?**
> "Hallucination, privacy/secrets leakage, prompt injection, over-reliance, and weak assertions that
> look comprehensive. I mitigate with context, redaction, review, and execution."

**A5. How do you prompt for good test ideas?**
> "Give the contract, business rules, and constraints; ask for a risk-ranked table with test level and
> oracle; separate ideation from code; ask for assumptions."

**A6. How do you use AI for locators?**
> "Ask for role/test-id first, with stability reasoning. Then verify uniqueness/actionability against
> the real DOM. The model can invent attributes, so inspection is mandatory."

**A7. How do you use AI for test data?**
> "Synthetic values, boundaries, locales, and invalid cases. Never real PII. I check validity,
> uniqueness, and domain constraints."

**A8. How do you use AI for failure triage?**
> "Provide the assertion, request/response, logs, trace summary, and recent changes. Ask for hypotheses
> with evidence and next steps, not a blind fix. I verify against the actual telemetry."

**A9. What is prompt injection in testing?**
> "Untrusted content — a web page, log, issue, or API response — contains instructions that hijack the
> model. I treat content as data, delimit it, and never let the model take actions without approval."

**A10. How do you handle privacy?**
> "Use approved tools, redact/synthesize data, never send secrets/PII, follow retention/residency
> policy, and review the provider's terms."

**A11. How do you measure AI usefulness?**
> "Accepted suggestions, review time, escaped defects, test runtime/flakiness, and developer
> satisfaction. Not 'tests generated.'"

**A12. What should not be delegated to AI?**
> "Risk ranking, business rules, compliance, security decisions, root cause without evidence, and
> final release approval. Those need human judgment."

**A13. How do you keep AI-generated code maintainable?**
> "Follow existing conventions, keep the public surface small, review like any PR, add tests for the
> test helpers, and avoid clever one-offs. Generated code still has to be owned."

**A14. How do you handle non-determinism?**
> "Pin model/prompt versions, keep prompts in version control, treat outputs as drafts, and rely on
> execution/review rather than reproducing the exact text."

**A15. What's a good first use case?**
> "Boilerplate with a clear spec and a fast verification loop: API client methods from OpenAPI, page
> object skeletons from stable DOM, or synthetic test data. Avoid high-risk domain logic first."

**A16. How do you prevent weak assertions?**
> "Review for meaningful oracles: status + body + state + side effect. Reject 'no exception' or
> 'status 200' only. Use mutation testing/coverage on critical logic."

**A17. How does AI fit into CI?**
> "It can summarize failures, suggest likely causes, and draft fixes, but CI gates remain deterministic:
> tests, thresholds, contracts, security scans. No LLM-only gate for critical releases."

**A18. What about AI-generated test data in reports?**
> "Mark it synthetic, keep it out of production, and ensure no real PII. Reports should be safe to
> share."

**A19. How do you review an AI-generated PR?**
> "Same as any PR, plus: verify invented fields/endpoints, check assertions, check secrets/PII, check
> dependencies, and confirm the change is actually needed. The author owns it."

**A20. What's the one rule?**
> "The model drafts; the human verifies and owns the decision."
